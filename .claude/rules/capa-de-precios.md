### Price layer (server)

Endpoints in `src/routes/api/` — `prices`, `search`, `resolve` (ISIN→ticker), `support` (Resend email). All rate-limited via `src/lib/server/rateLimit.ts` (Redis INCR/EXPIRE, permissive in-memory fallback).

- `api/prices/priceHelpers.ts` — Yahoo bulk quotes, plus **Financial Times HTML scraping** for funds Yahoo prices badly (`RELIABLE_FT_MAPPINGS`). The FT scraper regexes HTML and silently falls back to Yahoo when FT changes markup. Subunit currencies (GBp etc.) are normalized by `correctSubunitCurrencies()`.
- ⚠️ **FT-only funds (`src/lib/ft-assets.ts`) are the second FT path, and the reason they exist is share classes, not funds.** Yahoo carries *seven* share classes of Seilern World Growth but **not** the `EUR U R` one (`IE00B2NXKW18`), and searching that ISIN returns nothing at all. That matters far more than it sounds: classes of the same fund quote at wildly different NAVs — 116,01 € the `EUR U C`, 141,67 € the `EUR U I`, 463 € the `U R` — so pricing a holding with the wrong class is not a rounding error, it values the position at a quarter of what it is. "Yahoo has the fund" is not the same as "Yahoo has what this person holds".
- ⚠️ **That feature was implemented in three places by hand and broken in the one that mattered.** `/api/search` injected the ISIN with an inline `if`, `/api/resolve` had its own copy, `priceHelpers.ts` a third in `PURE_FT_TICKERS` that nothing read, and `ft-assets.ts` — the registry meant to govern it all — was imported by nobody. Four copies of one fund, and **`/api/prices` had none**, so the ISIN went to Yahoo like any other ticker, was not found, and the asset could be searched, resolved and added to a portfolio that then could not value it. Letting someone add what the app cannot price is worse than not letting them add it. All four now read the registry, so adding a fund is again what its own docblock promised: one entry, one place. ⚠️ **No safety net:** the `RELIABLE_FT_MAPPINGS` ones fall back to Yahoo when FT changes markup; these have nothing underneath, so a broken scrape surfaces as a visible entry in `errors`. And FT's summary page carries no history, so an FT-only asset has **no sparkline and no YTD/MTD/1M** — everything value-based (weights, deviation, maps, rebalancing, tax) works, the performance surface stays blank.
- ⚠️ **`searchFtAssets()` matches by word prefix and requires *every* term**, which is not fussiness: the comparison runs the opposite way to intuition — not "does the query contain the fund name" but "does the name contain the query" — so the original `includes()` matched `ow` against "Gr**ow**th" and injected a boutique fund into unrelated searches. Only an exact ISIN query puts it at the top of the results; a name match appends, because whoever types "world" is almost always after a global index fund.
- **Measured 7-ago-2026, and deliberately not acted on:** Yahoo now prices all three `RELIABLE_FT_MAPPINGS` tickers to within 0.0–0.6 % of FT (12,265 vs 12,27 · 13,716 vs 13,72 · 5,615 vs 5,58), i.e. the premise that "Yahoo prices them badly" may no longer hold, and the app is scraping three ~90 KB pages per price request for figures Yahoo already has. ⚠️ **One snapshot at one hour is not enough to remove them**: these are unlisted funds with a single daily NAV, and the classic Yahoo failure with them is *lag* — fine at 10:00, stale at 19:00. Sample across a day before touching it.
- ⚠️ **The FT scraper never extracted YTD, and could not have.** It searched `/Year to date.*?([\d,.-]+)%/s` in `/tearsheet/summary`, but FT publishes performance on a *different URL* (`/tearsheet/performance`); measured 7-ago-2026 against the three real tearsheets the app fetches, neither "Year to date" nor "YTD" appears in any of them, and "Performance" only occurs as a tab label. So `ytd` was always `undefined` and the `+server.ts` branch reading it was dead. Removed rather than repaired: YTD already comes from Yahoo history via `calculateHistoricalMetrics()`, and rescuing it would cost one extra HTTP request per ticker for a figure the endpoint already has. Price and daily change **do** match the live markup — verified against all three mappings the same day, including the Bitcoin ETP, where FT 302-redirects `/funds/` to `/etfs/` and `fetch` follows it.
- ⚠️ **A ticker Yahoo does not return used to fail in two different ways depending on the history cache.** Cold cache: the miss path threw and the message reached the client in `errors`. Warm cache: the hit loop was `if (hit.quote) push(...)` with no `else`, so the asset **vanished from the response** — no price, no error, nothing. The user neither controls nor can observe that cache state, so the same failure was reported or hidden at random. Both paths now report it. `server.test.ts` pins it with two sequential requests (the first warms the cache), and reverting the `else` fails exactly that test while the cold-cache one stays green.
- **The parsing is separated from the fetching on purpose** (`parseFTPriceHtml` vs `fetchFTPrice`, which takes an injectable `fetchImpl`): what is fragile here is not the network, it is the regexes, and FT can recompose its page any day. The fixture in `priceHelpers.test.ts` is **real markup captured on a dated day** — kept literal, tooltips and icon `<i>` included, because the value is precisely in what nobody would invent: the daily change is loose text (`-0.02 / -0.16%`) inside two nested spans. If those tests ever fail together, check whether FT changed the page, then recapture and **re-date the capture**.
- ⚠️ **`calculateHistoricalMetrics()` takes `now` as a parameter** for the same reason `calculateTaxAwareRebalance()` does: it is date arithmetic over three cutoffs (start of year, start of month, 30 days back), and a test written against the real clock passes today and fails in January — exactly when YTD matters.
- Redis (Upstash): only the **history/sparkline** is cached (`price_history:<ticker>`, TTL 4 h); live quotes always hit Yahoo. ⚠️ **The caching lives in `src/routes/api/prices/+server.ts`, not in `src/lib/server/redis.ts`** — that file is eleven lines that build the client and export `null` when the env vars are missing. This paragraph used to point at it, which sent anyone looking for the key or the TTL to the wrong file. The per-process fallback when there is no Redis is the `historyCache` object in that same endpoint. Without `KV_REST_API_URL/TOKEN` both cache and rate limiter degrade to per-process memory — effectively no caching/limiting on Vercel serverless.
- Currency pairs (`EURUSD=X`, …) are auto-appended to every price request; `CASH-*` tickers are synthetic (1.0 EUR).

#### `/api/fundamentals`: dividendos y resultados, y por qué NO van en `/api/prices` (25-ago-2026)

- ⚠️ **Todo esto ya llegaba y se tiraba.** La respuesta de `quote()` conserva nueve campos y descarta el resto; entre lo descartado estaban `earningsTimestamp`, `dividendYield`, `dividendRate`, `dividendDate` y `trailingThreeMonthReturns`. Rescatarlos **no cuesta ni una petición nueva**: es la misma llamada en bloque con los mismos tickers. Y el precedente de que un campo de fondo viaja gratis por ahí ya estaba: el TER sale de `netExpenseRatio`, que es un campo plano de la misma respuesta y **no** una llamada a `fundProfile`.
- ⚠️ **Y aun así no van dentro de `/api/prices`, que es la tentación obvia.** Esa respuesta se pide **cada 30 segundos** y esto cambia una vez al trimestre: meterlo ahí repite exactamente el error que este mismo fichero documenta haber corregido con el sparkline —«megabytes por hora de un dato que no se mueve durante la sesión»—. De ahí un endpoint propio, con **su bucket de rate limit** (no compite con los 30/min de precios), **caché de 24 h** en vez de las 4 del histórico, y una petición **bajo demanda**: la lanza la ficha al abrirse, así que quien no abra ninguna no gasta nada y quien abra cinco gasta una. Hay un test que se pone rojo si alguien los cuelga del sondeo.
- ⚠️ **`earningsTimestamp` solo existe para acciones.** Un fondo o un ETF no presentan resultados: el endpoint devuelve `null` para ellos **aunque la fuente mande una fecha**, y hay un caso que lo fija mandándosela a propósito. No es un dato que falte, es una pregunta que no aplica, y dibujar la casilla vacía inventaría una carencia en el 90 % de una cartera indexada.
- ⚠️ **La propia librería declara ±2 días de error** en esa fecha, así que la interfaz la rotula como aproximada en vez de presentarla como un hecho.
- ⚠️ **`dividendYield` llega unas veces en tanto por ciento y otras en tanto por uno.** Sin normalizar, la misma cartera enseña «1,4 %» o «140 %» según el activo y ninguna de las dos avisa. Se normaliza a fracción en el servidor.
- **Los activos de `ft-assets.ts` degradan a «no disponible» y ni siquiera llegan a la llamada.** No se les monta un scraper: son el único camino que valora esas posiciones, **no tienen fallback** por debajo, y el precedente de haber borrado el YTD de FT por costar una petición más está en `priceHelpers.ts`.
- **No hace falta tocar la CSP**, y conviene saber por qué: Yahoo y FT tampoco están en `connect-src` y funcionan, porque las llamadas salen del servidor. Mientras cualquier fuente nueva se consuma a través de un endpoint propio, `'self'` la cubre.
- ⚠️ **Lo que se descartó y no hay que volver a plantear sin releer esto: un feed de noticias.** Es lo más barato de todo —hay tres `newsCount: 0` escritos explícitamente en llamadas que ya se hacen—, pero la búsqueda de Yahoo es por texto y no por cartera, viene en inglés y sesgada a EE. UU., **no da nada para un fondo** —la mayoría de la cartera del usuario tipo— y choca de frente con la posición de producto que este repo declara tres veces sobre no fomentar la consulta diaria. Es el mismo argumento con el que se retiró el botón de refrescar.
