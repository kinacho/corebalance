### Look-through exposure and the maps

- `src/lib/lookthrough.ts` + `src/lib/data/indices.json` — what is actually inside the funds: regional and sector exposure, and overlap between positions ("your World and your S&P 500 point at the same companies"). Computed at **index level, never per holding**: real fund composition moves daily and needs a paid feed, while index weights move slowly and can be maintained by hand. Percentages are over **covered value**, not total portfolio, with `uncoveredValue` exposing what fell outside.
- **`indices.json` declares its own provenance.** Every index carries `regionsConfidence` and `sectorsConfidence`: `factsheet` (read off the official factsheet at `asOf`), `derived` (computed from other verified indices — the All-World is World + emerging), or `estimate` (order of magnitude, unverified). `calculateLookThrough()` returns `estimatedIndices` with only the unverified indices *that the user actually holds*, and the UI warns in orange. A test pins `msci-world` and `sp500` to `factsheet`. **When bumping `asOf`, re-check the factsheets and update the confidence flags** — the tests assert region and sector sums are 100 and that the World/US and S&P/tech weights match the published figures.
- `src/lib/treemap.ts` — squarified-ish treemap by binary splitting, ~30 lines, hand-rolled instead of pulling `chartjs-chart-treemap`: the maps are SVG, so they inherit `.privacy-blur` and the design tokens with no bridge, and it is deterministic and therefore testable (area conservation, no overlap, no infinite recursion on a dominant item).
- ⚠️ **The categorical palette in `constants.ts` is a validated artefact, not a taste choice.** `ASSET_COLORS` (six hues) and `CATEGORY_COLORS` (three) both pass all six checks of the `dataviz` skill's validator against the dark surface `#0d0d12`. The previous fifteen-hue palette **failed**: `#d946ef` and `#0ea5e9` sat next to each other and are indistinguishable under deuteranopia (ΔE 1.8), and eight of the fifteen were outside the lightness band. **If you touch either list, re-run the validator** (`node scripts/validate_palette.js "<hex,…>" --mode dark --surface "#0d0d12"` inside the skill). Non-obvious ordering constraints that fall out of it: green may not sit next to pink (ΔE 1.1 deutan) nor next to cyan (ΔE 11.8 even with normal vision), and blue may not sit next to violet (ΔE 0.4 deutan).
- **A seventh hue is never generated.** Past the six, charts fold the tail into an "Other" slice using `CHART_NEUTRAL`; `nextAssetColor()` reuses the least-used hue in fixed order. It used to fall back to `ASSET_COLORS[Math.floor(Math.random() * …)]`, which can hand out a colour already in the portfolio.
- ⚠️ **That rule lives in `src/lib/asset-colors.ts` now, because it was written twice and fixed once.** `AssetSearch.svelte` and `ImportModal.svelte` each carried their own `getNextColor()`; the random fallback was repaired in the first and left in the second, so **the CSV import path — the one that adds a whole portfolio at once, i.e. the only one that reliably goes past six assets — still handed out random duplicate colours.** Same shape as the FT-assets bug: one feature, several copies, broken in the copy that mattered. `assignAssetColors()` is the list version.
- ⚠️ **The demo portfolio hardcoded nine brand colours and bypassed the palette entirely** — `#00a4ef` Microsoft, `#4285f4` Google, `#555555` Apple, `#ff9900` Amazon, plus Tailwind defaults for the ETFs. Since the demo is what every first-time visitor and every screenshot sees, the example portfolio was the *worst* advertisement for the validated palette: three near-identical blues in one donut, and an asset painted the same grey as the "Otros" slice, so the chart showed two grey wedges meaning different things. It now calls `assignAssetColors()`. Two non-obvious details, both load-bearing: the assignment runs **across all three buckets at once** (per-bucket, core and stocks would both start at hue 0 and collide in the global-detail donut), and it runs in **expected-weight order, not declaration order** — the first six get the six distinct hues, and by declaration order Alphabet landed seventh, repeated IWDA's amber and the two showed up side by side.
- ⚠️ **The deviation treemap is sectioned by strategy block, and that is the fix for a whole family of colour problems — don't undo it.** **Targets are a core-portfolio concept**; satellite and stocks do not have them as such. The map used to squarify all three buckets onto one canvas and then flag two thirds of the assets as the exception ("sin objetivo") — assets that *structurally* cannot have a target. Every visual complaint it ever got came from that: first six of nine cells painted invisible, then a two-tone grey slab, then a legend carrying an entry that should not exist. None of it was a colour problem. It now squarifies **blocks by value first, then assets inside each block** (`layout` in `DeviationTreemap.svelte`; `squarify` places from the origin, so inner rects are translated into their block's slot). A block gets the diverging scale when **any of its assets has a target** (`measured`) — decided from the data, never from the block's name, so setting targets on satellites lights that block up on its own. A block without targets gets one flat hue from `BLOCK_HUES` and **no per-cell "sin objetivo" label at all**, because its header already says nobody expects a target there. The only thing still hatched is a genuine anomaly: an untargeted asset *inside* a measured block, which is the one case where a scale-less cell sits among scale cells with no gap or header between them. Two consequences of "measured is decided from the data" that were left half-done and are now handled, because the fresh-CSV portfolio (**every** asset at `targetWeight: 0`, so *nothing* is measured) is the first map most people see: the **subtitle** switches to `treemap.subtitle_no_targets`, since the usual one claims the colour is distance from target and there is no target to be distant from; and the **tooltip** has a third template, `tooltip_weight_only`, because "sin objetivo fijado" accuses the user of forgetting something that block never had. The header height is also **reserved whether or not the header is drawn** (`reservedH`, capped at a quarter of the block like the gap): subtracting it only when drawn meant a block could *shrink as the panel widened*, at the moment it crossed the "a header fits here" threshold.
- **The deviation map's midpoint is a low-chroma green, and both halves of that sentence are load-bearing.** Grey does not say "this is fine", but a saturated hue at a diverging midpoint is a genuine anti-pattern — it competes with the poles. `DEVIATION_ON_TARGET` (`#2f6b55`) is measured to sit in between: still **below the validator's categorical chroma floor** (so it reads as a near-neutral and does not compete) while being 3.2× more chromatic than the `#6b7280` it replaced. Numbers from the `dataviz` validator against `#0d0d12`, checked against what the map *actually paints* — the ramp floors, not the pure poles: normal-vision ΔE 17.7 vs the blue floor and 21.4 vs the amber, CVD 16.8 deutan / 11.8 protan. ⚠️ **The ramp still mixes from `CHART_NEUTRAL`, not from the green.** A ramp passes through its origin, and `mix(green, amber, 0.6)` is `#ac8136` — dirty olive. The small colour jump at the band edge is a feature: it marks the moment there is something to look at.
- ⚠️ **`CATEGORY_COLORS` cannot be reused inside the deviation map, however tempting.** `CATEGORY_COLORS.core` is *exactly* `DEVIATION_UNDER` and `CATEGORY_COLORS.stocks` is *exactly* `DEVIATION_OVER` — the categories donut and the deviation map already share two hex values with two unrelated meanings, in adjacent carousel lanes. So `BLOCK_HUES` is cyan / lime / violet (core / stocks / satellite), reusing only the satellite's violet. ⚠️ **It was emerald + cyan + violet and it had never been validated as a trio — emerald↔cyan is ΔE 11.8 normal-vision, the adjacency this very file declares forbidden, and a hard fail.** Not a corner case either: every asset from a CSV import is born with `targetWeight: 0`, so no block is measured and all three flat hues land on one canvas. The trio now passes all six checks all-pairs (worst: cyan↔violet ΔE 15.0 deutan, cyan↔lime 21.1 normal). **Which block gets which hue is also measured**, not arbitrary: the common case is a *measured* core, so the hues that coexist with the ramp floors are the other two, and lime is by far the best against the blue floor (31.2 normal / 29.5 protan, where cyan sat at 12.0 — a fail). Cyan therefore moves to core, which is almost never flat. Known, measured, accepted cost: violet vs the blue floor is ΔE 14.0 normal / 6.9 deutan, and lime vs the amber floor 4.3 deutan (16.8 normal, which does pass) — a failure **between different sections**, which is exactly what faceting addresses (visible gap, block header, per-cell labels) and the remedy the `dataviz` skill prescribes for all-pairs failures. Inside a single section nothing is under threshold, which is what was not true before.
- **The legend says only what the map could not say in place.** The three scale entries appear only if some block is measured; the pole swatches are **gradients** over `mix(neutral, pole, 0.6…1)`, because the map never paints the pure pole below ten points of deviation and a flat pole chip was more saturated than any cell on screen — that was a real "the legend doesn't match the map" bug. A block hue gets a legend entry **only when its header did not fit**: the conservative bucket is ~4 % of net worth, and in the narrow lane its block has no room for a header plus a cell, which used to leave an unexplained violet cell (and on mobile there is no tooltip to save it). Never both at once — a test asserts exactly that.
- ⚠️ **`text-transform` in CSS after measuring the string in JS is a bug, not a style choice.** The block headers were measured lowercase and rendered uppercase; uppercase is wider, so `approximateTextWidth` under-measured, and the `clipPath` — doing its job as the hard guarantee — cut mid-word: "ACCIONES INDIVIDUALE". The label is now uppercased in the script *before* measuring. **`letter-spacing` is the same bug one notch finer** and it survived the first fix: `.block-label` carries `letter-spacing: 0.04em`, which `approximateTextWidth` did not model, so the estimate ran ~4 % per character short. The function now takes a `letterSpacing` argument (em, like CSS) and `BLOCK_LABEL_TRACKING` mirrors the CSS value — three places that must move together, which is what the header-width test pins. General rule: CSS must not change the glyphs — **or their advance width** — after you have measured them.
- ⚠️ **SVG `<text>` neither wraps nor clips itself**, so a long label in a narrow cell paints straight over its neighbour. Two defences, and both are needed: `approximateTextWidth()` / `labelFits()` / `truncateToWidth()` decide *whether it is worth drawing* — with **per-character widths**, because a single average made all-caps tickers look 15% narrower than they render and `CASH-DEP` spilled out — and a `clipPath` per cell is the hard guarantee for when the estimate is wrong anyway. **Block headers need the same two defences**, and the test caught them missing the second: without a clip, "Cartera Conservadora" in the 4 % block paints over the block next to it. `DeviationTreemap.test.ts` asserts both invariants, on the desktop *and* the mobile geometry. ⚠️ **Two ways that header test was decorative, both fixed:** it compared each header against the widest cell in the *whole map*, so only the largest block could ever fail it; and it checked a single width, where the padding absorbs the `letter-spacing` error — it now sweeps twelve widths and asserts the label fits `blockWidth − 2 × inset`, which is what `truncateToWidth` actually promises (it fails at 360 px if the tracking is not modelled — verified by reverting the fix, not assumed). Also, **`svg.querySelector('#id rect')` does not work in these tests**: the map's clip ids are fixed per block, so a test that renders several times leaves duplicate ids in the document, the id selector resolves against the first render and then returns `null` for not being inside this SVG — a `null` that reads as "there is no clip".
- ⚠️ **Never compose a sentence by injecting another sentence into a placeholder.** `treemap.tooltip` used to take a `{target}` slot filled with either a percentage or the phrase "sin objetivo", which rendered as "**objetivo sin objetivo**". One template per case (`tooltip` / `tooltip_no_target` / `tooltip_weight_only`) instead — three, because the label that was rightly removed from the *drawing* of an unmeasured block stayed alive in `tooltipFor()` for months. The tooltip is where a retired wording hides.
- **The "Otros (N)" fold limit is one derived value, used twice.** `LookThroughMap` groups the tail of the ranking into a single slice, and how many it groups depends on whether the map is expanded (six hues' worth) or not (`MAX_CHART_SLICES - 1`). That number was written once for the fold and once for the label, so expanded the map grouped three regions and called them "Otros (4)". It is now `sliceLimit`, and the label reads `rawSlices.length - sliceLimit`.
- **The maps size their type from the container's real width, not from a media query.** A `viewBox` and an SVG `font-size` are attributes, so CSS cannot adapt them; and the deciding number is the *container* width, not the viewport — in its carousel lane a map is ~340 px **on desktop too**, which is why type tied to `matchMedia` rendered 9 px labels on a large screen. Both maps take `contentWidth` from `MapFrame` via `bind:clientWidth` and convert a target pixel size into viewBox units. In the look-through map, narrow lanes show **only the percentage** and the names are read from the ranking below, because "Consumo discrecional" truncated to "Consu…" informs nobody.
- **`MapFrame.svelte` is the shared shell of both maps**: header, subtitle, its own controls slot and the expand button. **Expanding widens the panel in place — it is not a modal.** It was one at first, and it brought every modal problem for no gain: it locked page scroll via `body.modal-open`, the lock survived closing with Escape so the page stayed frozen, and on mobile the media query that hid the expand button hid its own close button too. Widening in place has none of those states. The grid owns the width (`deviationExpanded` / `lookThroughExpanded` in `+page.svelte` flip `.map-box.is-wide { grid-column: 1 / -1 }`), because a grid item cannot leave its own track. Nothing else has to change on expand: the maps derive type size and canvas ratio from `contentWidth`, so widening the track recomputes everything. Note jsdom then needs a `ResizeObserver` — there is a stub in `src/setupTest.ts` — and tests pick a geometry by stubbing `clientWidth`, not by faking `matchMedia`.
- ⚠️ **The "no target" fill has failed twice, both times by being grey, and the deviation map now admits exactly one grey: the midpoint.** First it was white at 6 %, which over the dark surface is black — in any portfolio where only the core bucket has targets (the demo, and most real ones) six of nine cells vanished and the map looked broken. The fix was a second flat grey, and that failed differently: with six of nine cells grey the whole map read as switched off, and a second neutral also *means* the wrong thing, because it reads as "slightly more on target" when it means **not applicable**. The current answer splits the two cases, and the split is the whole point. A **whole unmeasured block** gets one flat hue from `BLOCK_HUES` and no texture at all, because its header already says nobody expects a target there. An untargeted asset **inside a measured block** — the genuine anomaly, the one case where a scale-less cell sits among scale cells with no gap or header between them — carries **hue + texture**: `UNTARGETED_FILL` (`#3b4250`, slate) with 45° stripes in `UNTARGETED_STRIPE` (`#5a6273`), so the two channels are independent and neither leans on hue discrimination. ⚠️ **This paragraph used to name `NO_TARGET_HUES` and `DARK_SURFACE`, and neither constant has ever existed in this repo** — it also had the stripe backwards, claiming it darkens toward the surface when `UNTARGETED_STRIPE` is *lighter* than its fill. Invented names in this file are worse than no names: they read as verified. `BLOCK_HUES` deliberately excludes **blue and amber** (they are the two poles, so a blue untargeted cell next to a blue "por debajo" cell is ambiguous) and **pink** (red reads as loss in a portfolio map, and pink beside green is ΔE 1.1 under deuteranopia — a forbidden adjacency of this palette). And "sin objetivo" is set apart by *italics*, not by a low alpha — white at 45 % was fine on dark grey and became illegible the moment the cells got a hue. Cells are labelled "sin objetivo" rather than showing a fabricated deviation (`deviation` is `weight − 0`, i.e. the weight again). The diverging mix starts at 60 % toward the pole, not 35 %, so a cell that has just left the band reads as coloured rather than grey. `DeviationTreemap.test.ts` asserts no fill is translucent or near-black **and resolves `url(#…)` to the pattern's own colours** — before that it read a fill it could not parse and silently passed on exactly the cells the check exists for.
- ⚠️ **The deviation map labels each cell with the asset's *name*, not its ticker, and that needed a module of its own (`src/lib/asset-label.ts`).** For a fund the ticker is its ISIN or Yahoo's `0P…` code, so the map was labelling positions `IE00B4L5Y983`. Truncating the name instead is *worse*: `iShares Core MSCI World` and `iShares Core MSCI EM IMI` share their first 18 characters, so both truncate to `iShares Core MS…` — the exact defect that killed the two donuts, one layout later. `assetLabelCandidates()` therefore returns **several labels from longest to shortest** and the component draws the first that fits whole (`shortestAssetLabel()` is what gets truncated when none does): the full name where there is room, then without the plumbing every fund carries (`UCITS`, `ETF`, the class in brackets, the class currency, `Inc`/`plc`), then without the provider, then one family word at a time (`Core`, `MSCI`, `FTSE`). ⚠️ **That last step exists because the test demanded it**: stripping only the provider leaves `Core MSCI World` against `Core MSCI EM IMI`, still nine shared characters. `FAMILIA` deliberately excludes `ESG`, `Value` and `Small` — those *do* distinguish two funds on the same index — and `cap` can never enter the plumbing list because it would eat `Small Cap`. Two consequences in the tests: cells are now found by a fragment of the name (how much name is drawn depends on the cell), and the satellite fixture had to be renamed, because it was called `Depósito remunerado` exactly like a core position and the `find` was picking the wrong block's cell.
- ⚠️ **Ordinary browser checks need one trick: `page.waitForURL` does not work for SvelteKit client-side navigation.** It waits for a `load` event that a client-side `goto` never fires, so it times out even though the URL changed. Poll `location.pathname` instead. Several hours were lost blaming the app for this.
- `DeviationTreemap.svelte` colours by **deviation from target, not daily change**. That is a product decision, not an oversight: daily colour teaches the user to check every day and react, which is the habit the app exists to prevent.

### Company-level concentration (18-ago-2026)

`src/lib/concentracion.ts` + the `companies` and `topHoldings` blocks of `indices.json`, drawn in `ConcentracionPanel.svelte`. It answers the one question the look-through structurally could not: **how much of your money ends up in the same company, counting funds and individual shares at once.**

- **Why the look-through could not answer it.** `findOverlaps()` crosses *pairs of positions that have an index*, so a loose share falls into `uncoveredValue` and disappears. ⚠️ **The demo portfolio was the worst case and it shipped that way for months**: an `IWDA.AS` in core plus Apple, Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet in stocks — four of that index's largest holdings — and the analysis said nothing. The i18n even admitted it out loud (`lookthrough.uncovered`: "acciones sueltas, efectivo…"), which is the tell that the gap was known and unclosed.
- ⚠️ **Every figure is a floor, and that is the property that makes it defensible rather than a hedge.** The dataset holds a top ten per index, so the tail is invisible: real exposure to a company can only be *higher* than what is computed, never lower. The panel says "al menos" and prints `coberturaPorIndice` — what share of each index it is actually seeing, from 21 % of the MSCI Europe to 78 % of the IBEX 35. A test pins the direction: what a fund's value distributes to must stay strictly below that value.
- ⚠️ **`holdingsSource` records the ETF each list was read from, and it is not decoration.** The figures come from physically-replicating trackers (`IWDA.AS`, `CSPX.AS`, `VWCE.DE`, `CNDX.AS`, `EMIM.AS`, `IMEU.AS`, `IJPN.L`, `LYXIB.MC`) queried on `holdingsAsOf`, so anyone can re-query that ticker and compare. A figure nobody can re-check ages without anyone noticing — the same reason `regionsConfidence` exists. Cross-check that was run: the Nasdaq 100 read from two different ETFs (`CNDX.AS` and `EQQQ.DE`) agrees to 0,01 %, which is what proves these are index weights and not one fund's quirks.
- ⚠️ **`msci-world-small` and the two bond indices carry no `topHoldings` on purpose, and the numbers are the argument.** In `IUSN.DE` the largest holding is 1,65 % and the whole top ten sums 3,5 %: a top ten of a small-cap index says nothing about concentration. And a government bond is not exposure to a company. A test asserts those three and only those three are absent, so adding companies to them forces the prose to change too.
- ⚠️ **The two identification signals collide in BOTH directions, so the precedence cannot be "one and then the other".** `resolveIndexKey` matches on names, so a share called *World Fuel Services* catches the `WORLD` and its money would be spread across the largest holdings of the MSCI World. But the mirror image fails identically: `resolveInstrumentType` returns `equity` for a suffixed ticker with no collective ISIN, so a fund like `ZPRV.DE` missing its ISIN reads as a share. What decides is whether the asset is *declared* a fund — an `indexKey` set by hand or by `normalizeAssets`, or a type that already says what it is; only with no declaration does the heuristic run, and there `equity` wins, being the more reliable of the two. ⚠️ **This was found by the tests, not by reasoning**: the first version checked equity first and four cases went red at once.
- ⚠️ **Share classes are merged into one company key, and ADRs too.** Alphabet A (2,31 in the World) and C (1,84) are one entry of 4,15 — for "how much Alphabet do I have" the class is irrelevant, and left apart the ranking would show two Alphabets of half the size. Hence `isins` is a **list**. Same for Samsung's ordinary and preferred lines.
- ⚠️ **`empresaDe()` resolves by exact ISIN and exact ticker, never by name, and the failure mode is why.** Matching names is how *Apple Hospitality REIT* ends up counted as Apple — and here a wrong match does not leave a visible hole, it moves money from one company to another in a figure the user reads as theirs. Every code was verified one at a time against the source; **Roche and TSMC are deliberately left with an empty `isins`** because their round trip did not confirm, and no ISIN is better than an unchecked one. The ISIN is also tried against the `ticker` field, because the CSV importers and the FT-only funds use the ISIN as the app's internal ticker.
- **`valorSolapado` is the value that arrives by the *second route onwards*** — for each overlapped company, its sources minus the largest one. With 6.000 € of Apple held directly and 2.470 € more inside a World, what overlaps is 2.470 €, not 8.470 €. Counting the total would inflate the headline, and the headline is the whole panel.
- ⚠️ **The fund-pair list moved out of `LookThroughMap.svelte`, and the reason is a denominator, not just visibility.** There it was capped at three rows (two when narrow), inside the second map, below the treemap and the ranking, behind a fold that starts closed on desktop. But the deciding argument is that its percentages are **over covered value** while this panel measures everything over total net worth: two similar-sounding sentences with two different denominators in two places is asking for a contradiction on screen. `findOverlaps` is now exported and reused — it has guards written from a mutation report, so rewriting it would have thrown those away — and `SolapamientoDeFondos` adds `pesoSobrePatrimonio` rather than overwriting `duplicatedWeight`, so neither number pretends to be the other.
- **The bond overlap rule that never existed is now declared** (`global-agg-bond` ↔ `euro-govt-bond`), which is why the conservative bucket could not previously produce a single finding, plus the two missing `ibex-35` pairs against the World and the All-World.
- **No new hue.** One bar per company in `--accent-blue`, and the "inside a fund / held directly" split is **text**, not two stacked segments: a two-colour segment would be a new pair needing the `dataviz` validator all-pairs against `#0d0d12` *and* `#ffffff`, and the text cannot fail an adjacency check. The bar is magnitude relative to the largest row, not percentage of net worth — with exposures between 2 and 8 % an absolute bar is a splinter in every row — and the exact figure sits next to it, so the bar carries no number.
- ⚠️ **The panel listens for `tour-step` with `abrir-concentracion`, and that is not only for the tutorial**: its header is a `<button>`, so it is the only way `contraste-vivo.mjs` and `e2e/tema.spec.ts` can reach the contents of a folded panel. Same mechanism `TaxAwareRebalance.svelte` uses, and the same reason the manage panel is opened that way.
- **The four branches its coverage does not reach are redundant, not unreachable**: a zero weight, a company key that does not exist, an index with no name. `concentracion.test.ts`'s integrity block forbids that state, so it would go red before the guard ever ran. They stay because the dataset is hand-edited.

### The panels that link to their lesson (13-ago-2026)

**Eight of the twelve panels** carry a link to the course lesson that explains them, via `LeccionDelPanel.svelte`, with the pairs in `src/lib/cursos-paneles.ts`. Before this the dashboard had **no outgoing link to any public content at all** (the logo was the only way out) and no contextual help beyond `title=` attributes, which do not show on touch — so on mobile there was none, and four panels did not even have that. It shipped as a three-panel pilot (`TimingCost`, `DriftChart`, `LookThroughMap`), was looked at on screen, and then got the other four.

- **The four left out, with the reason.** `CompositionBars`, `DonutChart` and `Projections` have no lesson that explains them. ⚠️ **`HistoryChart` has one and is left out anyway**: its match would be `twr-vs-mwr`, the lesson *before* `el-coste-del-timing` in the same course and about the same thing, and `HistoryChart` shares a card with `TimingCost` — two links to two consecutive lessons of one course, two hundred pixels apart, stop reading as "a lesson explains this" and start reading as noise.
- ⚠️ **In the four collapsible panels the link goes inside `.content`, not in the header.** Those headers are a `<button>`, so an anchor inside is invalid HTML and the button steals the click. It also means the link only appears once the panel is open, which is right.

- ⚠️ **Literal strings, never an import of `$lib/cursos`.** That module's `import.meta.glob` is **eager** and `CURSOS` lives in the same file, so importing it just to read a title drags the 34 compiled lessons *plus* the whole didactic kit into the **client** bundle (`/dashboard` is `ssr = false`). Neither of the two escapes this repo documents (`blog-locales.ts`, `related-reading.server.ts`) applies here: there is no server load to resolve the glob in. The price is a duplicated title, and `cursos-paneles.test.ts` is what pays it — it checks every entry against the real lesson through `getLeccion`, so a renamed slug or a reworded title fails instead of silently promising the wrong thing.
- ⚠️ **`target="_blank"` is mandatory, and not out of politeness.** `dashboard/+page.svelte` has a `beforeNavigate` that calls `portfolio.exitDemo()`, so **any same-tab outgoing link destroys the demo session**: whoever entered through "Ver la cartera de ejemplo", clicks the link and comes back finds an empty dashboard. And being a SPA, returning remounts all the local state (active tab, folded maps, chart range). **`PendingEditsPanel` is not the reason** — it has no `beforeunload`, and there is none anywhere in `src/`.
- **The link goes on its own line under the subtitle, not in the title row**: the drift lane is ~430 px, so the panel title plus the lesson title do not fit on one line. For the maps it is rendered by `MapFrame` through a `leccion` prop, because the subtitle belongs to that component — placed outside, the link landed *above* the subtitle.
- ⚠️ **Two defects that only measuring showed, not the screenshot.** `display: inline-flex` was not enough: inside a flex column (`.timing-card`) the child stretches, and the link measured **1216 px wide** — an invisible full-width clickable band. `align-self: flex-start` fixes it and the three now measure 216/240/265 px. And the arrow at `0.75em` of `--text-micro` rendered 9 px and did not read as an arrow.
- **The label is the lesson's title, never a generic "saber más"**: a generic label does not say what you are about to read, which is why it does not get clicked. The `LECCIÓN` chip supplies the context that it is part of a course.

### The two themes (15-ago-2026)

Started from three forum comments — *«la web necesita contraste como el comer»*, *«¿no hay modo claro?»*, and a third person who **defended the design and confirmed the contrast anyway**. That third one is what separated the two complaints: contrast is a measurable defect, the design is taste. Measured: **212 `color:` declarations below AA, 105 below even 3:1**, out of 719.

- ⚠️ **The tokens were fine and the code went around them, which is why this was invisible from inside.** `--text-muted` measured 5,42:1 and `--text-primary` 20,3. The scale had **two** rungs and no middle, so every component invented its own downwards — `rgba(160,160,200,0.6)` 40 times at 3,45:1. There are four rungs now (`--text-primary` / `-secondary` / `-muted` / `-faint`) and the *faintest* one still clears AA against the worst background. They are **solid hex, not alpha**: alpha composites with whatever is behind, so the same token measures differently everywhere and none of those is the number that was measured — and it compounds with a parent's `opacity` (the old muted under `opacity: 0.5` fell to 2,11 with nobody writing a new colour).
- ⚠️ **One hue, up to three tokens, because mark / ink / surface are three different thresholds.** `--accent-blue` fills an arc and only needs 3:1 against the canvas; as *text* it measures **3,22:1** and fails, hence `--accent-blue-ink`; as a *fill with text on it* what matters is the white inside, hence `--text-on-accent` (and `--surface-green` / `--surface-orange`, because white on `--accent-green` is 3,77 and on `--accent-orange` 3,19). Getting this wrong is not hypothetical: the migration sweep replaced `#3b82f6` (4,52) with `var(--accent-blue)` (3,22) in 33 places, i.e. **moving to the token lowered the contrast**.
- ⚠️ **`--bg-card` must stay `rgba(255,255,255,0.03)`.** Over `--bg-primary` it composites to **exactly `#0d0d12`**, the surface every ΔE figure in `constants.ts` and `chart-theme.ts` assumes. Raising it so cards read against the background (measured 1,05, i.e. nothing) would invalidate all of them at once — card legibility is the **border**'s job, which is also what WCAG 1.4.11 actually asks for.
- ⚠️ **The data palettes did NOT need light variants, and that was measured, not assumed.** `ASSET_COLORS`, `CATEGORY_COLORS`, `BLOCK_HUES` and the trend pair all pass the `dataviz` validator's six checks against `#ffffff` unchanged. The deviation map's own trio (`#4169c0` / `DEVIATION_ON_TARGET` / `#ad7537` — the ramp floors, not the pure poles) returns **byte-identical results on white and on `#0d0d12`**, ΔE 17,7 both ways; its one FAIL is the chroma floor on `DEVIATION_ON_TARGET`, which its own docblock declares deliberate. Running the check against the surface it is *already* validated for was the control that proved the light result was not caused by the light surface.
- ⚠️ **The theme cannot come from a cookie.** `hooks.server.ts` sets `Vary: Cookie` only on `/dashboard` and `/api` so public pages stay CDN-cacheable; a cookie theme would force it site-wide and drop that cache — most of the site and all of the SEO. It is resolved client-side by an inline script in `app.html` that writes `data-theme` **before first paint**, and `stores/theme.svelte.ts` only reads it from there. Without JS no attribute is set and `:root` serves dark, which is what the app always was.
- ⚠️ **Three places paint colour and cannot read a token, by construction**: the inline splash in `app.html` (it runs before any stylesheet exists — its light overrides need `!important`, because inline styles beat everything), `static/offline.html` (deliberately self-contained, so it gets its own `prefers-color-scheme` block), and the PWA manifest in `vite.config.ts`.
- **Chart.js reads the tokens at runtime now** (`cssVar()` in `chart-theme.ts`); `tooltipStyle`, `categoryAxis` and `valueAxis` expose their colours as **getters**, so a `{...tooltipStyle}` spread resolves them at chart-build time and no call site changed. Live switching goes through the `EVENTO_TEMA` DOM event and `seguirTema()` — an event and not an import, because importing `chart-theme.ts` from the theme store would drag Chart.js into the 72 prerendered public pages.
- ⚠️ **The hero mockup follows the theme, and getting there took being wrong first.** It was initially *pinned* to dark by redeclaring the tokens on `.hero-mockup` and `.floating-card`, on the argument that it is a *drawing of the app*, not UI. The argument is true and the conclusion was wrong: it is the landing's main image and takes up half the viewport, so in light mode it sat there as a black rectangle in the middle of a light page. **A drawing of the app in light mode is a drawing of the app in light mode.** Its text already used the scale; what had to go was the anchor, plus moving the surfaces to tokens (`--bg-overlay` for the frame, `--bg-card-hover` for the inner bands) and the shadow to `--mockup-shadow` — black at 60 % gives depth over a dark page and a dirty smudge over a light one.
- ⚠️ **The logo was a PNG and failed for two reasons at once, not one.** First: the raster carries **a dark navy outline and a drop shadow baked into the pixels** — measured, the file's four most frequent colours were that outline (`#104040` and family), not the mark. Over black it vanishes; over white it *is* the smudge. Second: the brand tones are neon and `#5eead4` measures **1,35:1 on `#f4f4f9`**, so in light mode the only thing that read clearly was precisely the dirt. It is now a vector (`assets/logo.svg`, traced from the master with contours **and holes** — without the holes each scale pan comes out solid, a filled teardrop instead of a drawn bowl, and the C's ring loses its double band) and the tones come from `--logo-from` / `--logo-to`. ⚠️ **That is why `Logo.svelte` inlines the SVG**: an `<img src="…svg">` cannot read the page's CSS variables, so it could not follow the theme. `npm run icons` renders the PNGs from that same SVG with resvg — each size is **rendered**, not downscaled, and the total went from 66 KB to 23 KB. The file itself uses a middle step (`#0d9488`→`#059669`, 3,4:1 on white and 5,4:1 on dark) because it goes where the background is unknown: the structured-data `logo`, the favicon, the `app.html` splash.
- ⚠️ **A per-block analysis misses the shape that hurts most: colour and background in different rules.** The tour's "Siguiente" button took its colour from a rule shared with "Anterior" and its background from the next one, so neither rule looked wrong on its own — and in light mode it rendered **black text on blue**. Same in `+error.svelte`. It is found by grouping on the last simple selector and checking whether, across all its rules, there is an accent background and a theme-following colour.
- ⚠️ **And a sweep that only measures what is visible on load leaves out half the app.** The first one set `corebalance_tour_seen` to skip the tour, so it never measured it; nor modals, nor panels. Measuring them turned up **159 nodes across 29 causes** — among them that **every modal had its dark surface hand-written** (`rgba(18,18,35,.95)` and family, 66 declarations in 23 files), i.e. a black panel with the theme's ink on top. The manage panel is opened from the harness with the app's own `tour-step` event, because its button is hidden in demo mode.
- ⚠️ **Three false positives that cost time and should not be chased again**: privacy mode sets the text `transparent` on purpose (it measures 1,03:1 and that is the feature, not a defect); `color-mix()` computes to `color(srgb r g b)` with 0..1 channels, which read as `rgb()` gives an absurd ratio; and its alpha travels after a slash (`/ .12`), so a 12 % tint is mistaken for a solid accent. All three made the output shout at correct code, which is how you end up not reading the output.
- ⚠️ **Three defects lived *only* below 1024 px, and the reason they survived is structural: mobile styles are in the BASE rule and desktop overrides them.** I had counted colour declarations inside `@media (max-width: …)` — 17 in 156 blocks — and concluded the mobile risk was small. Wrong question: `.history-section` and `.charts-row-card` declare their background, border and `box-shadow: 0 8px 32px rgba(0,0,0,.4)` in the base rule, and `@media (min-width: 1024px)` sets them to `none`. At 1440 px, where everything was measured, they are never painted. The three: `.hamburger-bar { background: #fff }` over a `--bg-card` button — **white on white, and it is the only way into the nav below 1140 px**; `.tabs-sticky-nav` with `#05050a` hand-written (the dark `--bg-primary`) in a `sticky` element; and those two shadows, now `--card-shadow` / `--pill-shadow`.
- ⚠️ **`UpdatePrompt` and `InstallPrompt` were the last two components pinned to dark with Tailwind utilities, and no sweep can reach them**: one needs the service worker to announce a version, the other needs `beforeinstallprompt`. Not even opening every modal gets you there. Forced from the harness with `ui.swUpdateReady = true` and a fake `deferredPrompt`.
- ⚠️ **`var(--token, <literal>)` is a literal in disguise, and there were 114 of them.** The fallback only fires if the token is missing — so today it is dead code, and the day someone renames a token the UI drops **silently** to a value below AA instead of breaking visibly. `BlogPost.svelte`'s fallback was exactly `rgba(160,160,200,0.6)`, the value this repo has on file at 3,45:1. They are gone; `contraste.mjs` now warns about any that come back.
- ⚠️ **Two colour decisions had escaped into JS, where no CSS analyser looks.** `CompositionBars`' `stateColor()` returned `DEVIATION_UNDER`/`_OVER` — the treemap's **fill** palette, which only needs 3:1 against the canvas — as inline text colour (2,91:1). And `AssetCard`'s `.target-badge` used `var(--accent)`, the asset's own `ASSET_COLORS` hue, as text (3,35:1). Both are the mark/ink split again. ⚠️ **Only the e2e with a seeded portfolio found them**: the demo is exactly on target, so neither badge is ever painted with demo data.

- ⚠️ **Two guard holes that let three invisible figures through, and both are the same mistake in different clothes: the check decided what to look at by a proxy instead of by the thing itself.** Found 16-ago-2026, reported by a *user* — under the global capital, the per-block breakdown in `HeroSummary.svelte` carried `style="color: #fff"` in the markup, which in light theme is white on the `#ffffff` of `--bg-card`: **1,00:1** measured, three figures literally not there. The `€` suffix of the cash card had the same shape at 1,10:1. Three guards existed and not one of them could see it.
  - **The static one looked only inside `<style>`.** `scripts/contraste.mjs` builds its haystack with `cssDe()`, so a colour in an attribute was out of scope by construction — the same shape already on file for the two colour decisions that escaped into JS, one paragraph up. It audits the markup now.
  - ⚠️ **The two live ones skipped `.privacy-blur`, and that class is on every money figure in the app.** The exclusion was written for privacy mode, which sets the text `transparent` on purpose and measures 1,03:1 — a real false positive, listed three paragraphs up. But the class sits in the markup **always**; what paints transparent is `.privacy-mode .privacy-blur`, and that case was *already* covered by the next line, which drops anything whose computed colour is transparent. So the exclusion was redundant in the case it was written for and destroyed the coverage in the case it was not: `contraste-vivo.mjs` and `e2e/tema.spec.ts` were both structurally blind to the figures that matter most in a portfolio app. They discard by **colour, not by class**, now. ⚠️ Negative control: with the defect back in place the widened sweep names the three at 1,00:1 and the e2e goes red; before the change that same test passed with them invisible.
  - The fix itself is `--text-primary` from a class: in dark it measures 20,34:1 exactly as `#fff` did, so the arithmetic only moves in the theme that was broken.

- ⚠️ **What only the browser caught, twice.** The static analyser and a CSS read-through both missed that `.landing-page` painted `background: #05050a` opaque over a perfectly correct `--bg-primary` — the whole light theme rendered black, 234 text nodes under threshold, while every token resolved right. And 165 backgrounds plus 159 borders written as translucent white (`rgba(255,255,255,0.03…0.08)`) simply vanish on a light surface: the dashboard's four metric cards became four figures floating with no box. Neither is visible in a declaration; both are visible in a screenshot.

### Styling & UI conventions

- `src/routes/layout.css` is the **only** CSS entry point (Tailwind v4 via `@import 'tailwindcss'`; there is no `tailwind.config.js`). Design tokens are CSS variables in `:root` (`--bg-primary`, `--bg-card`, `--text-primary`, `--text-muted`, `--accent-blue/green/orange`, `--state-positive/negative`, `--chart-grid/axis/ink/surface`, …).
- ⚠️ **`--accent-blue/green/orange` used to be `#3b82f6` / `#10b981` / `#f59e0b` — Tailwind's blue-500, emerald-500 and amber-500 untouched, i.e. the most recognisable default palette on the internet, and the reason the app read as generic no matter what the charts did.** They are now the same values as `CATEGORY_COLORS`, which are validated against the card surface `#0d0d12`. The split mattered because it was **semantic, not just cosmetic**: the donuts painted "Acciones" with the validated amber and `HistoryChart` painted the same category emerald green, one carousel lane apart. One hue per entity, defined once.
- Reuse the existing convention classes instead of reinventing them: `.privacy-mode`/`.privacy-blur` (blurs figures in privacy mode), `.shimmer` (skeletons), `.asset-card`, `.background-mesh`, `.noise-overlay` (uses the `#noiseFilter` SVG declared in `app.html`), `body.modal-open` (scroll lock).
- ⚠️ **`body.modal-open` is put on and taken off by `src/lib/modal-lock.ts`, which *counts*. It used to be five hand-written copies and two of them were wrong, one in each direction.** `LedgerModal`'s `onDestroy` was an **empty block** with a comment explaining that it must not release the lock because `ManageAssets` is behind it — true in one of the three places it opens from, and false in the two that matter: from `AssetCard` and `CompactAssetRow` there is nothing behind, so closing it left the `body` at `overflow: hidden` plus the window's height, i.e. the dashboard with no scroll and clipped. That is what "the page half-breaks and doesn't come back" is. The mirror image: `ImportModal` released it **always**, and it only ever opens from inside `ManageAssets`, so closing it let the page scroll under the still-open panel. Counting answers both without any modal having to guess what is behind it — which is what those two comments were trying to do. `desbloquearScroll()` never goes below zero, because a double release would leave the next real lock ineffective. Same defect this file already records for `MapFrame`, which was a modal and dragged the lock along when closed with Escape.
- `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` is applied to a **closed list of classes** (`.metric-value`, `.summary-value`, `.asset-value`, `.total-value`, …) — a new class that displays figures must be added to that list or the digits jitter.
- ⚠️ **The colour of a *state* is a different layer from the colour of an *entity*, and mixing them is what made the dashboard look like six different products.** `STATE_POSITIVE` / `STATE_NEGATIVE` in `constants.ts` (mirrored as `--state-positive` / `--state-negative`) are reserved for up/down and are never a series; `ASSET_COLORS`, `CATEGORY_COLORS` and `BLOCK_HUES` are identity and never mean "good". Before this, **green meant six things at once** — acciones, money in, profit, positive return, on-target and "con DCA" — across five different greens and four different reds picked per component. The fix was subtraction: the surviving pair is two hexes that were already in the repo, and it measures **ΔE 12,0 deutan** where the worst co-occurring pair used to be 6,5. The measured cost is written in the `STATE_POSITIVE` docblock: it sits at ΔE 6,0 from `ASSET_COLORS[5]`, which is only defensible because state colour is always *text with a sign* and never a mark.
- **Chart chrome lives in `src/lib/chart-theme.ts`, chart numbers in `src/lib/chart-format.ts`.** The first holds `applyChartDefaults()`, `tooltipStyle`, `categoryAxis`, `valueAxis` and `motionAllowed()` — the three Chart.js canvases each carried their own diverged copy, and only the donut's tooltip declared the project font, so **every axis in the app rendered in the system sans**. The second is the axis/estimate number format. ⚠️ **`formatCompactCurrency()` takes the grid `step` and it is not optional decoration**: decimals are decided by the step, not the magnitude, because a portfolio moving between 116.000 € and 116.500 € otherwise writes `116k €` on all six gridlines. `stepFromTicks()` extracts it from what Chart.js hands the tick callback; `niceTicks()` does the same job for the hand-drawn SVG charts. `formatEstimate()` drops the cents — a 20-year projection quoted to the céntimo is invented precision that reads as a promise.
- **`HistoryChart` has a fourth mode, `split`: your money against what the market added.** Two stacked bands — contributed below, growth above — answering the question no net-worth chart answers: *is this going up, or have I just put more in?* The data was already in the store (`invested` / `gain`); what existed was a fifth stepped line that shipped switched off, i.e. the answer hidden behind a toggle. ⚠️ **In this mode the y axis starts at zero and that is not a preference**: with the axis starting at 60k — correct for a line, where what you read is the shape — the bands stop being proportional to what they are worth, and 64.265 € of a 116.052 € portfolio rendered as a 10 px strip under a huge slab. A stacked area whose axis misses zero is not an imprecise chart, it is a chart saying something else. Both this and the legend contradiction (the "what you put in" swatch drawing hollow because `localStorage` still had it switched off, while its band was painted) were found by **looking at the screenshot**, not by reading the code. The two fills come from `CONTRIBUTED_FILL` / `MARKET_FILL` in `chart-theme.ts`, shared with the projection cone so that past and future do not use different hues for the same idea.
- **The net-worth line is drawn green above what you contributed and red below, segment by segment** (`TREND_UP` / `TREND_DOWN` in `chart-theme.ts`) — per segment and not one colour for today's state, because that is the difference between informing and decorating: it shows *when* you were down, which a single-colour line hides. The reference depends on the mode: in euros it is the contributed capital, in return or gain it is zero, and in the stacked mode it does not apply because there the line is the edge of a band whose meaning the two fills already carry.
  - ⚠️ **They are NOT `STATE_POSITIVE`/`STATE_NEGATIVE`, and that is deliberate.** That pair's whole defence, written in its own docblock, is that it appears "in text and badges, never as the fill of a cell or an arc". A line is a mark, so the defence does not apply — and measured with the `dataviz` validator against `#0d0d12`, `STATE_POSITIVE` (#34d399) **fails the lightness band** (L 0,773, too light to paint on the dark surface). Reusing it would have been free to write and false to look at.
  - The chosen pair passes **all six checks** alongside the three `CATEGORY_COLORS` that can be drawn at the same time: green↔red ΔE **32,0** normal / **8,6** deutan; red↔the amber of "Acciones" **18,8** normal. ⚠️ The obvious candidates died there: `#dc2626` sits at **14,4**, under the hard floor of 15, and `#e11d48` drops to 5,8 deutan against the green. Green↔amber is a 7,9 protan WARN, legal only with secondary encoding — which exists, because this chart always carries a legend.
  - ⚠️ **The legend swatch had to follow**, and it did not at first: it stayed white next to a green or red line — the same legend-contradicts-chart defect as the "what you put in" band, found the same way, **by looking at the screenshot**. It now takes the colour of the last segment, i.e. today's state.
  - ⚠️ **The green cannot be `#059669`, and that took a second pass**: it is exactly `ASSET_COLORS[2]`, the emerald an asset is painted with. Reusing it would have handed the dashboard back the defect this project fixed by subtraction — "green meant six things at once" — because `CompositionBars` paints assets from that palette one row over. `#047857` is a darker step of the same family, outside `ASSET_COLORS`, and passes the six the same (worst pair: red↔green ΔE 8,4 deutan).
  - ⚠️ **And validating it turned up a pre-existing hard fail that is now fixed too: `CATEGORY_COLORS.satellite` was `#7c3aed`, ΔE 0,4 from `core` under deuteranopia.** This same file forbids blue-next-to-violet for `ASSET_COLORS`, and there it was — with both drawn as **simultaneous lines** in this chart. The donut got away with it on ordering (the amber sits between them, so adjacency holds); **a line chart has no adjacency** — series cross and share space, so the pair has to be measured all-pairs, and there it was 12,4 in normal vision, under the floor of 15. It is `#a21caf` (magenta) now: **9,7 deutan and 22,6 normal**, same purple family so the satellite is still recognisable, measured against the other two category colours *and* against the green/red of the net-worth line — the five series that can share that canvas. ⚠️ `BLOCK_HUES.satellite` deliberately **does not** change: that trio is a separate system chosen for how it coexists with the diverging ramp, and it already diverges from this one on its other two entries (cyan vs blue, lime vs amber). Measured against the ramp's blue floor, the magenta improves normal vision (14,0 → 19,9) but worsens deutan (6,9 → 3,2), so there it would be a trade rather than a fix. Here it is a fix on both.
- ⚠️ **«No es una pérdida» was written for outflows and was being said on contributions too.** The clarification exists for a real reason: when you sell, net worth drops and the chart looks like it recorded a loss, so the tooltip denies it in words. But the condition was `if (flow)` — *any* flow — so a contribution read **«Aportación: 1.000 € — no es una pérdida»**: absurd on its face, and it spends the clarification on the case that does not need it, devaluing it for the one that does. Exactly the trap this file already describes for tooltips: **they are where retired wording survives.** Two lines now, and the amount is written in **absolute value** because `formatEUR` of a negative flow gave «Salida: −24.000,00 €» and the sign repeats what the word already says. The decision moved to `flowTooltipLine()` in `chart-format.ts` so it can be tested — it decides what the user is told in two cases that are not the same — and `flowLabel()` was deleted rather than left as dead code.
- ⚠️ **`HistoryChart` drew its line with `tension: 0.45`, and a spline does not interpolate — it overshoots.** On a net-worth chart that is peaks and troughs of money that never existed, drawn by the curve engine rather than by the data. It is `0` now, in all three line charts. Related and deliberate: an inflow is a **filled** dot and an outflow a **hollow** one — same hue, shape carries the difference — because the outflow used to be painted amber next to a tooltip footer written expressly to say *a sale is not a loss*. Its ranges are also no longer 7D/14D/30D but 1M/3M/YTD/1A/Todo defaulting to **Todo**: a maximum horizon of one month says the same thing this file elsewhere forbids the deviation map from saying.
#### La columna de herramientas: una concha, una abierta a la vez, dos grupos (19-ago-2026)

Salió de la pregunta «¿qué te parece el dashboard a nivel visual?». Lo que abarataba esa columna no era la falta de efectos: eran **cinco fichas con el mismo peso visual**, tres de ellas sin decir ni una cifra en su estado cerrado. Seis ideas se midieron contra el código y **cuatro estaban mal valoradas**; lo que sigue es lo que quedó en pie.

- **`src/lib/components/PanelHerramienta.svelte` es la concha de los cinco paneles**, y sale de que `.panel`, `.panel-header`, `.panel-info`, `.panel-icon`, `.panel-title`, `.panel-subtitle`, `.chevron`, `.collapsible`, `.wrapper` y `.content` estaban escritos **cinco veces** (513 líneas). ⚠️ **Las divergencias del duplicado eran exactamente las reglas que hacen falta para que quepa una cifra**: `gap` en la cabecera, `min-width: 0` en el texto y `flex-shrink: 0` en icono y chevron solo existían en los dos paneles que ya la tenían. Añadirla a los otros tres sin portarlas reproduce el defecto de los 117 px de `MapFrame` que este mismo fichero documenta. Mismo movimiento que `MapFrame` hizo con los mapas.
- ⚠️ **`.panel-header` es un nombre con consumidores fuera del componente** y no se puede renombrar: lo clican `e2e/panel-fiscal.spec.ts` y `scripts/capturar-graficos.mjs`. `.sidebar-item`, en cambio, no lo usaba nadie y ha desaparecido.
- **Una herramienta abierta a la vez**, con el estado en `dashboard/+page.svelte`. ⚠️ **Lo que compra no es estética: es que `.sidebar` ya era `position: sticky; top: 6rem` y estaba decorativo**, porque un elemento pegajoso más alto que la ventana no tiene recorrido. El peor caso pasa de la **suma** de los cinco al **mayor** de los cinco, y el `max-height: calc(100dvh - 8rem)` con `overflow-y: auto` cierra el caso de los dos simuladores, que solos ya no caben. ⚠️ **El `top: 6rem` NO es el número equivocado y no hay que «arreglarlo»**: la cabecera mide ~81 px y 65 al hacer scroll. Y el `overflow` va en el propio elemento pegajoso, que es inocuo; **en cualquier ancestro mataría el sticky**.
- ⚠️ **La selección única volvió vacua una guarda del tutorial, y ese es el defecto menos guessable de todo el cambio.** `prepararDestino()` en `OnboardingTour.svelte` espera a que el destino tenga `height > 0` antes de dejar avanzar a driver.js — y una cabecera plegada siempre la tiene (~85 px medidos), así que la espera se cumplía al instante y el globo medía la cabecera mientras el cuerpo se abría debajo: exactamente el defecto que esa función existe para arreglar, entrando por la puerta de atrás. La concha pone `data-abierta` y la espera lo consulta; los destinos que no son plegables no lo llevan y se saltan la condición.
- **Dos grupos con rótulo, «tu cartera» y «simular», y la razón es de honestidad, no de maquetación.** ⚠️ **Proyección y Crisis no tienen ningún dato de la cartera que enseñar**: sus cifras salen de deslizadores locales **no persistidos** (7 % / 15 % / 20 años / 500 €, y −40 % / 7 %). Así que las tres primeras llevan `.cifra` —dato del store, con `privacy-blur` si es dinero— y los dos simuladores llevan `.supuesto`: sus supuestos en tono apagado, sin píldora y sin tinte de estado. #### The mobile sweep (10-ago-2026): what a screenshot cannot tell you

Started from "the net-worth charts don't look right on mobile". Measured with `scripts/auditar-movil.mjs` at 390×844, and the three defects it found are three different kinds of invisible.

- ⚠️ **The charts tab left the document 431 px wide inside a 390 px window, and «Todo» — the default range — was the one button off-screen.** The two control groups of `HistoryChart` sat in one row: four mode buttons plus five range buttons, each with `min-width: 32px` and `0.7rem` of horizontal padding, in a flex row that could not give. So the whole page scrolled sideways and the range you were actually looking at was unreachable. **A screenshot cannot show this** — what overflows is precisely what is not in the picture — which is how it survived so long. Fixed with one row per group and `flex: 1; min-width: 0` on the buttons, which also takes the touch targets from 32 px wide to about 70.
- ⚠️ **The guard for that needed its negative control run twice.** `e2e/movil-sin-desbordamiento.spec.ts` asserts the document never exceeds the window on any tab, and that every `.range-btn` ends inside the screen. Reverting the column layout left it **green**, because either half of the fix suffices on its own; only with the whole media query disabled does it fail — and then it says the number (`«Todo» acaba en el píxel 431`). Defence in depth working, but whoever removes one half will not hear it from the suite.
- ⚠️ **`--text-micro` in `layout.css` exists because nine components wrote their own smallest size and all nine were unreadable on a phone.** Measured: between `0,55rem` and `0,62rem`, i.e. **8,8 to 9,9 real px** — «Capital Global» inside the donut hole at 8,8, every card's ISIN and TER at 9,8, the axis captions at 9,9. It was not one component's slip, it was a convention with no floor. The token is `0,6rem` and rises to `0,7rem` **only below 640 px**: on a desktop those sizes are deliberate and work. Two of them were `clamp(0.55rem, 2.5vw, 0.65rem)`, where the floor sat exactly where a phone always lands. Deliberately **not** touched: the miniature dashboard drawn in the landing hero (`Hero.svelte`), whose 8,3–9,6 px labels are a decorative mock-up of the app, not text anybody reads.
- ⚠️ **An SVG with no `width` fills whatever box you give it.** The stacked-mode icon looked right only by accident, because its button was 32 px; widening the buttons stretched it to 88 px and its two bands read as two loose grey bars. The size of an icon cannot depend on how wide its button happens to be.
- Also fixed: the comparison links on the landing were **clipped, not scrolled** (a `white-space: nowrap` link inside a centred paragraph had nowhere to fall, so the last one lived past the right edge and was unreachable), and a handful of landing/footer links that were 16–18 px tall got padding.
- ⚠️ **The map header put its text column at 117 px, and two complaints that sound unrelated were both that number** (16-ago-2026). `.head` in `MapFrame.svelte` is a flex row with `.head-text` and `.head-actions`, and the second carries `flex-shrink: 0` — so the region/sector switch kept whatever width its content asked for and the text column took what was left: **117,2 px** measured at 390. Out of that: the lesson link measured **305,5 px** inside it, overhanging by 188; and the two pills came out at **73,4 px**, which wraps «Por región» onto two lines and grows the button to 52,1 px tall. It is reported as *«los botones se ven demasiado grandes»* and the cause is that they are too narrow. Note `.mode-switch` already declared `width: 100%` with a comment about «dos objetivos grandes» for the thumb — it could never hold, being 100 % of a narrow sibling. Below 640 px the header stacks and the 316 px lane is the text's and the switch's in turn: pills go to **136,8 × 44,0** on one line. The stacking is gated on `con-acciones`, set by `MapFrame` only when the `actions` slot has something, because the deviation map does not use it and would just gain an empty row.
- ⚠️ **And the lesson link's ellipsis had never once been applied.** `.titulo` in `LeccionDelPanel.svelte` declares `overflow: hidden; text-overflow: ellipsis; white-space: nowrap` with a comment saying that in a narrow lane the title truncates instead of pushing the panel — except `.leccion-link` is `inline-flex` with `align-self: flex-start`, both of which size it to its content, and a flex child is born with `min-width: auto`. Nothing ever forced `.titulo` to shrink, so those three lines were dead code and the link simply overflowed. `max-width: 100%` on the link plus `min-width: 0` on the title is what makes the documented behaviour actually happen — a comment describing something that does not occur, which is a shape this repo has hit before.
- ⚠️ **None of that overflows the *window***, which is why `e2e/movil-sin-desbordamiento.spec.ts` swept over it for months: the link escapes its **container**, not the screen. Its new case asserts against each element's own container, and both negative controls were run and are independent — without the stacking the pills fail, without the `max-width` only the link does. A third assertion ("the label does not wrap", counting `Range` rects) was **removed**: the probe works in isolation (at 48 px it returns 2) but with the real defect present it still returned 1 while the height already gave it away, so it was an assertion that did not catch what it claimed to.
- ⚠️ **The history chart's range selector: the arithmetic was right and the control was still broken, and getting that distinction wrong cost a round trip.** Reported twice, the second time as «a veces no hay reacción». The first investigation stopped at "not a defect" because the maths checks out — with a seeded year of `corebalance_holding_edits` and long sparklines, 1M/3M/YTD/1A/Todo give **five different pixel signatures** on mobile and desktop alike, and the collapse to one view is the honest consequence of `ventanaHistorica` being 30 days without a ledger. All true, and all beside the point: **a button that cannot do anything is a defect of the interface even when the calculation behind it is correct.** Measured across history depths, the tail of ranges always collapses — at ~45 days `YTD`, `1A` and `Todo` draw the same canvas, so three of five buttons were dead — and the notice did not cover it, because ⚠️ **`ALL` never warns by design** (it promises no number of days, so it cannot lie): the real sequence was tapping `1A`, reading «todavía no hay tanto historial», tapping `Todo`, seeing the same chart **and watching the explanation disappear**.
  - `isRangeRedundant()` in `range.ts` decides it, and the component **stops drawing** the redundant ranges. ⚠️ **Not `disabled` with a `title`, which is what it was first**: dimmed means `--text-faint` (`#8a8aa3`) against the live `--text-muted` (`#a8a8c0`), two adjacent rungs that barely read apart side by side — and `title` does not show on touch, which is precisely where this was reported. A control that neither looks disabled nor can explain itself is worse than absent.
  - ⚠️ **It is NOT `clipNoticeFor(...) === 'short'`, though the first version was.** The two questions separate exactly at the edge: a range covering the series *exactly* has nothing to warn about — no day is missing — yet still shows what `Todo` shows. And that is the common case, not a corner: without a ledger the window is 30 days flat and `1M` asks for 30. Hence `<=` here and `<` there, and neither can delegate to the other. **The unit suite passed on the wrong version; the e2e caught it by comparing pixels.**
  - ⚠️ **Below two live ranges the whole selector goes**, which is the fresh-portfolio case. Disabling alone left a row of five buttons with four dead — not more honest than before, just a different way of looking broken. There the note carries it alone.
  - ⚠️ **Any spec that measures those buttons must call `sembrarHistorial()` first** (in `e2e/util/cartera.ts`), and pick its depth deliberately: 400 days for the five-button geometry, 200 for the filtering. `movil-sin-desbordamiento.spec.ts` broke on exactly this and its failure read «"1M" no tiene caja» — pointing at the locator instead of at the cause.
  - **Measured and deliberately not acted on**: at the scroll offsets where the range selector passes under `.tabs-sticky-nav`, hit-testing a button's centre returns the tab bar, and a tap there switches tab. It is normal sticky behaviour and `.mobile-tabs` is opaque (`rgba(15,15,20,0.95)` plus a blur), so the buttons are not visible there and nobody aims at them — checked on a screenshot before discarding it. Recorded so the next person does not re-investigate it.
- ⚠️ **What the audit flagged and is NOT a defect, so nobody chases it twice:** the SVG maps report `font-size` in *viewBox units* (3,5 / 4,6), which the browser then scales — the rendered sizes are the intended 13 / 11 / 10 px. And `.background-mesh` overhangs the viewport on purpose; being `position: fixed`, it does not extend `scrollWidth`.

#### El icono del activo (19-ago-2026)

- ⚠️ **`src/lib/asset-icon.ts` existe porque el icono decidía mal y nadie lo medía**, y sale de `utils.ts` por la misma razón que salió `asset-label.ts`: decide algo y no tenía ni un test. Lo reportó el autor —«tengo activos que no son defensivos y sale un escudo»— y al mirar su cartera real eran **cuatro de cinco activos con el icono equivocado**.
- ⚠️ **La causa raíz son las abreviaturas, no las reglas.** Yahoo y FT sirven «iShares Dev Wld Idx (IE) S Acc EUR» y «iShares EmergMkts Idx», no los nombres completos: `WLD` no contiene `WORLD` ni `EMERGMKTS` contiene `EMERGING`, así que ninguna regla geográfica casaba y ambos caían en la última red —«es un fondo»— que devolvía `🛡️`. Un escudo sobre un indexado global no es un icono neutro: se lee como «defensivo», que es justo lo contrario. Ahora el escudo se reserva a los fondos de **defensa**, que es su único sitio honesto.
- ⚠️ **`indexKey` va antes que el nombre.** La app ya sabe qué índice replica cada fondo —el importador lo trae de la columna «índice» del bróker y el usuario puede corregirlo—, y esa señal estaba ahí sin usarse: los dos fondos del escudo ya traían `msci-world` y `msci-emerging` guardados en disco.
- ⚠️ **Las palabras se buscan enteras, y eso obliga a listar las variantes.** `includes()` a pelo casaba `ETH` dentro de `NETHERLANDS`, `SOL` dentro de `SOLVAY`, `TECH` dentro de `BIOTECH` y `ORO` dentro de `TESORO`. El coste del cambio es real y su propio test lo destapó: `BIOTECHNOLOGY` no contiene la palabra `BIOTECH`, así que «iShares Nasdaq Biotechnology» se iba a tecnología hasta que se añadió la variante.
- ⚠️ **No hay banderas, y es una medición y no una preferencia.** Chrome en Windows no dibuja los emoji de bandera: los pinta como sus dos letras indicadoras. Medido en el navegador del autor sobre producción — `🇺🇸` mide 30,7 px y `🇪🇸` 26,9 frente a los 43,9 de cualquier emoji real, o sea dos glifos de texto estrechos donde el resto de la columna lleva un icono. La familia de globos (`🌐` global, `🌎` América, `🌍` Europa, `🌏` Asia y emergentes) dice lo mismo, se dibuja en todas partes y evita elegir un tópico nacional por país. **Misma decisión que la 1.19.0 tomó para la columna de herramientas**, y por el mismo motivo: un emoji lo pinta la fuente del sistema.
- ⚠️ **`normalizeAssets()` recalcula el icono siempre, no solo cuando falta.** `Asset.icon` guarda un glifo pero **no hay pantalla donde el usuario lo elija**: es una caché de lo que decide la función, y respetarla congelaba en disco el resultado de la versión que lo guardó —en esa cartera real sobrevivía un `⚛️` que ya no existe en ningún sitio del código—. Si algún día hay selector de icono, esto vuelve a ser un relleno y la elección del usuario se guarda aparte, como ya pasa con `indexKey`.
- **Lo que sigue sin cubrir**: los iconos siguen siendo emoji, así que su trazo lo pone cada sistema operativo. Pasarlos a SVG dibujados —como los cinco de la columna de herramientas— obliga a que `Asset.icon` guarde una **clave** y no un glifo, con migración de lo guardado y seis sitios de render que tocar; es un cambio de su propio tamaño y no entró aquí.

#### El formulario del libro se cerraba solo, y el patrón se repetía en otros cinco sitios (25-ago-2026)

Reportado con un GIF: abres «+ Añadir» en el libro de un fondo, empiezas a escribir y a los pocos segundos el formulario se cierra solo y borra lo escrito. Medido fotograma a fotograma antes de tocar nada —155 fotogramas a 10 fps— para separar lo que pasaba de lo que parecía: no se cierra el modal, se cierra **el formulario**; y el movimiento ya guardado sigue en la lista, así que no había pérdida de datos.

- ⚠️ **La causa: un `$effect` cuya dependencia era la *identidad* de un prop y no el *valor* que debía dispararlo.** El de `LedgerModal.svelte` reinicia el formulario cuando cambias de activo, y leía `asset.ticker`. Pero `asset` no es un objeto suelto: llega por una cadena de getters —`asset` ← `position.asset` de `AssetCard.svelte` ← la fuente del `{#each}` de `PortfolioSection.svelte`—, así que la dependencia real del efecto es esa fuente, no el `Asset`. Cada recálculo de `portfolioState` escribe una `position` nueva ahí y el efecto vuelve a correr **aunque `position.asset` sea exactamente el mismo objeto**. La clave del `{#each}` evita el remontaje de la tarjeta; no evita esto. La guarda es por valor: se compara el ticker contra el último mostrado y se sale si no cambió.
- ⚠️ **El comentario que había encima afirmaba que `untrack()` protegía justo de eso, y era falso.** `untrack()` protege los *valores* que se leen dentro (ahí, el precio por defecto); no la dependencia del prop. Otro comentario que describe algo que no ocurre — la misma forma que este fichero ya documenta para `LeccionDelPanel.svelte`.
- **La segunda vía, y es desperdicio general:** `applyTerUpdates` hace un `map`, o sea devuelve un array nuevo **aunque no haya actualizado nada**, y `fetchPrices` lo asignaba siempre. Con eso, cada 30 s se invalidaban `coreAssets`, `satelliteAssets` y `stockAssets` —y con ellos `portfolioState`, `ledgerHoldings`, los dos mapas y los gráficos— sin que hubiera cambiado un dato. Ahora solo se reasigna si hubo actualización.
- ⚠️ **El mismo patrón, buscado a propósito en los 29 `$effect` de `src/`, apareció en cuatro sitios más.** El que hacía daño hoy está en el importador y se cuenta en su propia regla. Los otros tres: `PortfolioSection.svelte` tenía un `$effect.pre` escribiendo el «abierto/cerrado» de la sección desde un prop que **nadie pasa** —código muerto hoy, el mismo defecto mañana, porque ese componente recibe `portfolioState`—, y es una inicialización; `Projections.svelte` guardaba su input de capital con el **modo** en vez de con «el usuario ya escribió esto», así que lo tecleado se perdía al volver del otro modo; y `CrisisSimulator.svelte` metía dos dependencias independientes en un efecto. **Descartados con su motivo** para no perseguirlos dos veces: el efecto que sincroniza el modo libro (idempotente, relee la verdad del store), el que rellena el precio al abrir el formulario (depende de un estado local, que es la condición correcta), los volcados a Chart.js y los dos *gatekeepers* con `goto`.
- ⚠️ **En jsdom no se puede probar que una capa se coma un clic, y creerlo cuesta una prueba decorativa.** El calendario del formulario tenía un fondo de cierre con `position: fixed; inset: 0` y `z-index` por encima de todo el panel: con el calendario abierto, **lo único clicable de la pantalla era el calendario**, y guardar exigía dos clics. Se sustituyó por un listener de `pointerdown` en `window` —el patrón que `Header.svelte` ya usa para su menú—, que cierra el calendario **y** deja pasar el clic. Pero jsdom no tiene disposición ni prueba de impacto, así que allí un elemento superpuesto no intercepta nada: esa mitad vive en `e2e/libro-de-operaciones.spec.ts`.
- ⚠️ **Y en Playwright, `locator.click()` tampoco vale para medirlo: reintenta.** Con la capa puesta, el primer clic se lo comía el fondo, el fondo se cerraba y el reintento llegaba al botón — verde con el defecto delante. Un clic por coordenadas sí falla, pero mide una caja que en la tanda en paralelo se queda obsoleta y acaba aterrizando en el fondo del modal: rojo intermitente por el motivo equivocado. Lo que decide sin depender de animaciones es preguntar con `elementFromPoint` quién ocupa el botón, con un desplazamiento a la vista antes, porque el cuerpo del modal tiene su propio scroll.
- ⚠️ **Las secciones de cartera nacen plegadas**, así que cualquier spec que toque una `.asset-card` tiene que desplegarlas primero. Sin eso el clic falla con «`section` intercepts pointer events», que apunta al localizador y no a la causa. Mismo motivo por el que existe `abrirMapas()`.
- **Convenciones de modal, comparadas las nueve capas del repo.** `LedgerModal.svelte` era el único modal real sin Escape, sin `focusTrap` y sin ningún atributo ARIA; ya los tiene, con el Escape dando precedencia al calendario. ⚠️ Y eso destapó un fallo **vivo** en `ManageAssets.svelte`: su guarda de Escape enumera a mano los hijos y se dejaba el importador, así que —los handlers de `window` no se consumen entre sí— salir del importador con Escape llamaba además a la cancelación del panel, que restaura el estado original: **se descartaban todos los cambios pendientes de la sesión de gestión**. El patrón de guardas a mano no escala; con tres hijos aún es legible, y la salida el día que haya un cuarto es contar, como `modal-lock.ts`.
- **`AssetSearch.svelte` y `SupportModal.svelte` no bloqueaban el scroll**, los dos únicos. El primero se salvaba porque siempre se abre desde dentro de `ManageAssets.svelte` — el razonamiento que el docblock de `modal-lock.ts` documenta como equivocado, y el que dejó a `LedgerModal.svelte` sin soltar el bloqueo. El segundo se monta sobre la página y sí movía el fondo.
- ⚠️ **Desde la vista de tarjeta, el libro era inalcanzable en ciclo cerrado.** El único abridor de `AssetCard.svelte` estaba dentro de un `{#if}` sobre el modo libro, y el único interruptor para *activar* ese modo vive dentro del modal que no podías abrir. Las otras dos vías no lo condicionan (`CompactAssetRow.svelte` abre con el icono del activo, `ManageAssets.svelte` usa `class:active`), y las dos vistas son intercambiables en el mismo sitio: la misma acción estaba o no según el modo de vista. Ahora el icono del activo abre el libro también en la tarjeta, reusando la convención de la fila compacta.
- **Polirrelleno nuevo en `src/setupTest.ts`: `Element.prototype.animate`.** jsdom no trae la Web Animations API y las transiciones de Svelte 5 se apoyan en ella, así que cualquier componente con una transición reventaba al renderizar — lo que dejaba fuera del alcance de las pruebas de render precisamente a los modales. ⚠️ Tiene que **avisar de que terminó**, en un `setTimeout` porque Svelte engancha su limpieza después de crear la animación: un doble que no avisa deja los nodos de salida colgando, y entonces comprobar ausencia falla sobre código correcto. Por eso las aserciones de «el formulario está abierto» se leen del botón —que es el estado— y no de si el nodo sigue en el DOM.

#### El rótulo de «lo que más lo mueve hoy», y el botón de refrescar que sobraba (25-ago-2026)

- ⚠️ **La lista de movers rotulaba con `tickerLabel()`, y ahí ese criterio está mal.** Esa función prefiere el ticker y solo cae al nombre cuando el ticker no dice nada —un ISIN, un código de Yahoo—, con el argumento de que un ticker es corto, exacto y es como el usuario llama a su posición. Vale para una celda de treemap. No vale para una lista que contesta a «qué está moviendo tu dinero hoy»: ahí un ticker obliga a traducir antes de entender la respuesta, y quien no se los sepa de memoria no la entiende en absoluto. Lo reportó el autor mirando su propia cartera.
- **`descriptiveAssetLabel()` es el nombre sin fontanería, conservando la gestora**: «Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS ETF» → «Vanguard FTSE All-World», «Fidelity MSCI World Index Fund» → «Fidelity MSCI World», «Apple Inc» → «Apple». Además de descriptivo es **más corto que el nombre entero**, así que trunca menos que la alternativa evidente.
- ⚠️ **No es `assetLabelCandidates()` en su segundo elemento, que era el atajo obvio y está mal.** Esa lista se deduplica, así que cuando el nombre no lleva fontanería el segundo elemento pasa a ser el nombre **sin gestora**: «iShares Core MSCI World» se serviría como «Core MSCI World». La gestora es media identidad de un fondo —es lo que distingue dos que replican el mismo índice—, así que aquí se conserva siempre. Hay un caso en `asset-label.test.ts` que lo fija, con el candidato equivocado escrito al lado para que se vea la diferencia.
- **Medido en pantalla**: a 390 px los tres rótulos caben enteros en su columna (166–168 px), así que el truncado no llega a activarse en el caso normal.
- ⚠️ **El test que fijaba el ticker se reescribió, no se borró**: un cambio de contrato tiene que verse en la prueba que lo guardaba. Y se le añadió el caso que impide volver atrás — que dos tickers perfectamente legibles **no** aparezcan en la lista.
- ⚠️ **El botón de refrescar de la cabecera se quitó, y el comentario que queda en su sitio explica por qué; no lo devuelvas sin leerlo.** No hacía nada que no estuviera pasando ya: los precios se sondean solos cada 30 s y al volver a la pestaña se piden de inmediato, y encima vienen de una caché en Redis, así que pulsarlo no traía nada más fresco. El único momento en que un refresco manual cambia algo es tras varios errores seguidos —ahí el sondeo se espacia hasta cinco minutos— y ese caso **ya tenía su propio control**: el «Reintentar» del banner de error del dashboard, que además aparece justo cuando sirve. La señal de «esto está vivo» tampoco se pierde: la hora de actualización con su punto latiendo, el texto de «actualizando» y la barra de carga están en `Header.svelte`, dos elementos más arriba. Y el argumento de fondo ya estaba escrito en este mismo fichero: el mapa de desviación colorea por distancia al objetivo y no por el cambio del día porque el color diario enseña a mirar cada día y reaccionar, que es el hábito que la app existe para evitar. Un botón de refrescar es el gesto de ese hábito, puesto en la cabecera. Sobrevive la animación `spin`, que la usa el indicador de carga del avatar.

#### La ficha del activo (25-ago-2026)

Salió de «me gustaría dar más información al usuario sobre las acciones que lleva… ¿cómo lo enfocarías?», con una restricción suya: **no sobrecargar las tarjetas**.

- ⚠️ **El punto de partida no era «qué datos traer» sino «qué sabe ya la app y no cuenta».** El caso extremo: `canBeTransferred()` en `instrument-type.ts` contesta a «¿puedo traspasar esto sin tributar?», está documentada, tiene tests y **no la llamaba nadie**. Y el índice que replica cada fondo solo aparecía dentro de un `<select>` de edición, teniendo `indices.json` la frase de cobertura, el top diez real y el reparto por región y sector.
- **La superficie ya existía**: desde la 1.20.2 el icono de cualquier activo abre su modal, con `focusTrap`, `role="dialog"`, Escape y bloqueo de scroll ya pagados. Para un activo sin libro estaba prácticamente vacío. Nada de esto toca la tarjeta.
- **`ficha-activo.ts` es un módulo aparte y no código dentro del componente**, por el criterio de siempre: decide cosas de dinero. Y porque `LedgerModal.svelte` ya eran 964 líneas antes de empezar.
- ⚠️ **Sin libro no se calcula la plusvalía, y eso se dice.** `buildFifoLots()` necesita transacciones; sin ellas devuelve `[]`. El módulo devuelve un estado `sin-libro` explícito en vez de un cero: un cero ahí se lee como «no debes nada» cuando lo que pasa es que no se sabe — el defecto que este repo arrastra documentado desde el importador que metía activos a coste 0 y fabricaba una ganancia del 100 %.
- ⚠️ **Dos pestañas, «Ficha» por defecto**, y el estado de la pestaña **se reinicia en el efecto guardado por ticker**. Desde `ManageAssets` este modal cambia de activo sin remontarse: sin reiniciar, te enseñaría el libro de un activo bajo el nombre de otro. Es el mismo efecto que esta misma mañana cerraba el formulario solo, y la lección generaliza: **cualquier estado nuevo de ese modal tiene que entrar en ese bloque**.
- ⚠️ **Los porcentajes van por `$LL.dashboard.percent()` y no por `formatPercent()` de utils.** Aquella multiplica por 100 y formatea con `toFixed`, o sea con **punto decimal**: en una app en castellano pinta «0.12%». La de i18n recibe una **fracción**. Aquí llegan las dos formas —el TER es fracción, los pesos del dataset van de 0 a 100—, así que hay que dividir donde toca. Lo cazó un test que esperaba «1,4» y encontró «140.00%».
- ⚠️ **Las claves de `topHoldings` son identificadores en minúscula** (`nvidia`, `alphabet`), así que la primera versión pintó la lista de mayores posiciones en minúscula. Se resuelven contra el registro `COMPANIES` de `concentracion.ts`, que es donde vive el nombre correcto. **Lo encontró una captura, no un test**: todas las pruebas miraban pesos y orden, que estaban bien.
- **La frase larga va debajo del nombre y no en la columna de la derecha.** Puesta a la derecha se lleva casi todo el ancho y lo que se trunca es el nombre —«iShares Core S&P 5…»—, que es justo lo que hay que leer. A la derecha solo la cifra, que además se alinea entre filas.
- **`etiquetas-indice.ts`** saca los rótulos de las nueve regiones y los once sectores de `LookThroughMap.svelte`, que era su único consumidor hasta que apareció el segundo. Se construyen como objetos y no como un `switch` con `default` a propósito: así una clave nueva del dataset la caza el test de integridad en vez de salir en pantalla como texto crudo.
- ⚠️ **La fuente va escrita, y solo en el bloque que le corresponde.** La ficha tiene **cuatro procedencias distintas** y rotularlas todas «Yahoo» sería falso: lo del índice se lee de un ETF de réplica física y ya lo dice con su ticker y su fecha; lo fiscal sale del libro del propio usuario y se rotula como estimación con FIFO; el solapamiento se calcula sobre el dataset curado a mano. Lo único que viene de Yahoo en vivo son los dividendos y los resultados, y era lo único que no lo decía. Lo pidió el autor con el motivo exacto: si una cifra no cuadra con la del bróker, que se sepa de quién es el dato. Un test fija que «Yahoo Finance» aparece **una sola vez**, para que nadie lo reparta por bloques que no le tocan.
- **Y cuando no hay datos se nombra a quien no los cubre**, en vez de un genérico «no tenemos estos datos» — que se lee como un fallo de la app cuando es cobertura de la fuente. Aplica a los activos de `ft-assets.ts`, que no están en Yahoo.
- ⚠️ **El coste anual del activo faltaba en `CompactAssetRow.svelte`**, y estaba en la tarjeta desde siempre: cambiar de vista hacía desaparecer una cifra sin que nada lo dijera, y la compacta es justo la que se usa para comparar posiciones entre sí. La guarda es un e2e que compara **la misma cifra en las dos vistas** en vez de comprobar que existe en cada una: así también se pone rojo si una de las dos la calculara distinto. Va en tono apagado porque es contexto y no resultado — la misma distinción que la columna de herramientas hace entre una cifra de la cartera y un supuesto.
