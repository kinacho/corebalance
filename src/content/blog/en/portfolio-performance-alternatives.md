For DIY investors who are passionate about personal finance and tracking every penny, **Portfolio Performance** has been the go-to open-source tool for years. It is a robust, free, and extremely powerful desktop application that allows you to calculate metrics like TWR (Time-Weighted Return), MWR (Money-Weighted Return), and graph your net worth.

However, despite its strengths, Portfolio Performance suffers from a major issue: **its steep learning curve and visual complexity**.

For passive or Boglehead investors whose main goal is to contribute to 2 or 3 index funds or ETFs monthly and perform an occasional rebalancing, setting up and maintaining this desktop tool is like cracking a nut with a sledgehammer.

In this article, we will analyze the best **alternatives to Portfolio Performance**, comparing their pros, cons, and which one you should choose based on your actual financial tracking needs.

---

## 1. Why Look for an Alternative to Portfolio Performance?

Although it is a favorite application in passive investing forums, Portfolio Performance has clear practical limitations:

* **Steep Learning Curve:** Its interface looks like a Bloomberg terminal. Configuring assets, importing transaction statements, and fixing decimal mismatches requires hours of studying tutorials.
* **Desktop-Only:** There is no official mobile app or native web version. Your data is saved in a local file (`.xml` or `.portfolio`) on your hard drive, making it difficult to quickly consult from a mobile phone or tablet.
* **Unnecessary Complexity for Rebalancing:** The software is designed for historical return and dividend analysis, not for providing a quick, simple solution to "how to distribute my $500 contribution this month."

## 2. The Main Alternatives on the Market

Let's review the available options to replace it, categorized by tool type:

### Alternative A: Custom Spreadsheets (Google Sheets / Excel)
This is the classic, most widespread alternative. It consists of building your own template using formulas or functions like `=GOOGLEFINANCE` to pull market prices automatically.
* **Pros:** Absolute flexibility. You can design the tables and charts exactly as you wish.
* **Cons:** Requires constant manual maintenance. If you switch index funds or if the API fails (which is common for European ISINs), your formulas will break and you will have to repair them yourself.

### Alternative B: Robo-Advisors and Managed Portfolios
If you delegate your portfolio to a robo-advisor or use the summary screen of the bank where you hold your index funds:
* **Pros:** Total automation. You don't have to do anything since the platform buys, sells, and rebalances for you.
* **Cons:** You are tied to their specific funds and terms. In addition, robo-advisors charge an extra management fee (usually around 0.15% to 0.50% annually), which reduces your long-term returns. They also do not allow you to unify assets from different platforms into a single view.

### Alternative C: Web Calculators Focused on Rebalancing (CoreBalance)
Modern web applications designed for independent Boglehead investors who manage their own portfolios across traditional brokers or low-cost platforms (like Interactive Brokers, DeGiro, or Vanguard).
